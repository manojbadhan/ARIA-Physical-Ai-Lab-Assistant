## 1. Short answer for the robot

This is a **Creality HALOT-MAGE S 14K**, a desktop **resin 3D printer** that uses **LCD MSLA** technology. A UV light source shines through a high-resolution monochrome LCD screen onto liquid photopolymer resin, hardening it layer by layer. The build plate lifts the model out of the resin tank (vat) as it grows.

It is used for very detailed small parts: miniatures, figurines, jewellery patterns, dental/medical models (with suitable resins), and precision prototypes. Printed models come out **wet with uncured resin** and need washing and UV curing before use.

## 3. How does it work?

1. **Slicing:** A model (STL or OBJ) is imported into a slicer such as HALOT BOX or CHITUBOX. The slicer adds supports, cuts the model into layers, and exports a print file.
2. **Transfer:** The print file reaches the printer by **USB drive** (two USB ports on the front), **Wi-Fi**, or **Ethernet** (rear), depending on setup.
3. **Printing:** The build plate lowers into the resin vat, which has a clear release film on its base. The LCD shows each layer's image and the UV light cures the resin in that shape. The plate then lifts and the next layer is exposed.
4. **Finish:** The plate rises fully, and the model hangs on the plate, wet with resin.

Key features listed by the manufacturer include a 10.1-inch **14K monochrome LCD**, an **Integral Light Source 3.0**, a **"Dynax+" fast mode** (up to 150 mm/h), a high-speed **"Pictor" release film**, a **laser-carved build plate**, a **"MageArch" flip lid**, and a **built-in air purifier** with activated carbon filtration.

## 6. General operating guide

This is a **general guide for the HALOT-MAGE S 14K**. It is not a substitute for the user manual or the resin manufacturer's instructions. Follow the supervisor's lab rules.

### Before starting
1. Place the printer on a **stable, level surface** away from direct sunlight and strong light, in a **ventilated** room. Leave space around it.
2. Wear **nitrile gloves**, **safety glasses** and suitable clothing. Uncured resin can irritate skin and eyes and can cause allergic reactions. Read the resin's **Safety Data Sheet (SDS)**.
3. Check that the power cable is undamaged and not on the floor where it can be tripped over or pulled. Use a properly rated, grounded socket.
4. Check that the lid, vat, release film, and build plate are fitted and undamaged. The LCD screen beneath the vat must be clean and dry.
5. Keep the printer away from food, drinks, and children.

### A. Preparing the printer
1. Check the **build plate is levelled** (the plate-levelling method uses four screws and is done with a sheet of paper; the printer is usually pre-levelled at the factory). Re-level if prints do not stick or after moving the printer.
2. Check that the **vat has a clean release film** with no tears or cloudy cured resin lumps.
3. Shake or stir the resin bottle as the manufacturer recommends. Pour resin into the vat **slowly and only up to the marked fill level**. Do not overfill. Avoid air bubbles.

### B. Printing
1. Slice the model in a slicer with the correct **resin profile and exposure settings** for your resin. Make sure the model has supports and is angled sensibly.
2. Save the file to a USB drive and insert it.
3. Close the orange lid fully.
4. Select the file on the touchscreen and start. Do not open the lid during printing, apart from an emergency pause.
5. Check the first few layers if the lab rules allow. A failed first layer wastes resin.

### C. Removing the print
1. Wait for the plate to rise and let resin **drip back** into the vat for a few minutes.
2. Wearing gloves, take off the build plate carefully. Use the correct scraper (plastic or the supplied tool) to remove the model. **Do not use sharp metal tools on the release film or the LCD.**
3. Place the model in the wash step (LAB-OBJ-001). Do not leave it on the bench or touch it bare-handed.
4. Wipe resin from the plate with a paper towel and a suitable cleaning liquid, and put used towels and gloves in the **resin waste container**.

### D. Cleaning and storage
- If the vat will not be used again soon, **pour the resin back through a filter** into its bottle and clean the vat with a soft tool and the appropriate cleaning liquid. Do not scratch the film.
- Close the resin bottle tightly and keep it out of sunlight.
- Put the lid on and switch off the printer at the end of a session.

## 7. Safety precautions

- **Uncured resin is hazardous.** Avoid skin contact. If it gets on skin, wash with soap and water at once and follow the SDS. Seek medical advice if irritation continues.
- **Ventilation:** Resin smells and releases vapours. Use in a ventilated area. The built-in activated-carbon air purifier reduces smell but is not a replacement for ventilation.
- **UV light:** Never look at the UV light or the LCD while it is on. Do not remove or bypass the orange lid.
- **Waste:** Do not pour resin, cleaning liquid, or contaminated water down a sink or drain. Collect it as hazardous waste. Liquid resin should be cured in sunlight or a UV chamber before disposal only if local rules allow it. Ask the lab supervisor.
- **Flammable cleaning liquids** (for example isopropyl alcohol) must be kept away from flames, sparks, and hot surfaces.
- **Electrical:** Disconnect power before inspecting or cleaning the printer. Do not open the housing. Do not use it if the cable, plug, or housing is damaged. Keep liquids away from the electronics.
- **LCD damage:** The LCD can be damaged if resin leaks onto it or if a sharp tool touches it. If resin gets under the vat, stop and tell the supervisor.
- **Moving parts:** Keep fingers away from the plate and arm while the Z axis is moving.
- **Misc.:** Do not leave a printer unattended unless lab rules allow it. Follow the lab supervisor's instructions for resin handling.

## 9. Basic troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible checks |
|---|---|
| Printer does not power on | Check the wall outlet, power strip, and cable. Do not open the housing. |
| Touchscreen is blank | Check power, wait for boot, and power-cycle. The screen in the photos is dark, which may simply mean the printer is off. |
| USB file not found or won't load | Use a correctly formatted USB drive. Use the file format the printer and slicer expect. Try another port or drive. |
| Print does not stick to the plate | Re-level the plate, clean the plate, check the first-layer exposure, and check the resin temperature (cold resin sticks poorly). |
| Print sticks to the film instead of the plate | Increase bottom-layer exposure, check levelling, and check for supports on the first layers. |
| Missing layers or holes in the print | Check the LCD for damage, run the screen/light test, check for cured resin bits in the vat, and check the exposure settings. |
| Vertical stripes or layer lines | Check the Z-axis for loose screws or dirt, check the plate is tight, and reduce the speed. See the manufacturer's FAQ. |
| Cloudy film or lumps in the vat | Filter the resin, replace the film if damaged, and clean the vat. |
| Resin leak onto the LCD | Stop. Switch off. Tell the supervisor. Wipe only as directed and do not run the printer. |
| Strong smell | Check ventilation, the lid, and the air-purifier filter. Check for a resin spill. |
| Strange noise, smoke, or sparks | Stop, disconnect power if safe, and report it to the supervisor. |

Do not dismantle the printer or the LCD assembly as a student operator.

## 12. RAG-ready question and answer pairs

**Q: What is this object?**  
A: It is a Creality HALOT-MAGE S 14K, a resin (LCD MSLA) 3D printer. It cures liquid resin with UV light through a 14K LCD to build very detailed models.

**Q: Is it the same as the wash and cure machine?**  
A: No. The wash and cure machine (LAB-OBJ-001) cleans and UV-cures finished resin prints. The HALOT-MAGE S makes the prints. Both have orange covers, which can cause confusion.

**Q: Is it the same as the filament 3D printer?**  
A: No. The filament printer (LAB-OBJ-002) melts plastic filament. This printer uses liquid resin and UV light.

**Q: What does "14K" mean?**  
A: It refers to the LCD's very high resolution, 13320 × 5120 pixels. It allows fine details, with a pixel size of about 16.8 × 24.8 µm.

**Q: How large can a print be?**  
A: Up to about 223 × 126 × 230 mm.

**Q: Why is the lid orange?**  
A: It blocks UV light and protects the resin from room light. Keep it closed during printing.

**Q: What are the two USB ports for?**  
A: They accept USB drives with print files. Check the manual for the exact purpose of each port.

**Q: Why is the screen dark?**  
A: In the photos the printer appears to be switched off, so the screen is not lit.

**Q: How do I start a print?**  
A: Level the build plate, add resin to the vat up to the fill mark, slice a model, load the file by USB, close the lid, choose the file on the touchscreen, and start.

**Q: What do I do with the print afterwards?**  
A: Wearing gloves, remove the plate and model, then wash it (LAB-OBJ-001 Wash mode), let it dry fully, and cure it (Cure mode).

**Q: Is the resin safe to touch?**  
A: No, not when uncured. It can irritate skin and eyes. Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection, follow the SDS, and wash the skin immediately if it is contaminated.

**Q: How do I dispose of resin waste?**  
A: Do not pour it down a drain. Collect it as hazardous waste and follow the lab supervisor and local rules.

**Q: What are the white bits on the bench in front of the printer?**  
A: They are probably scraps of cured resin or support remnants. They should be collected and disposed of properly. Do not assume they are safe if they are wet.

**Q: Where can I find the manual?**  
A: On Creality's download page for the HALOT-MAGE S: https://www.creality.com/uk/download/creality-halot-mage-s
